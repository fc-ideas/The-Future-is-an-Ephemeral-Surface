# Less AI-Like Writing

Use this skill when prose feels generic, over-smoothed, repetitive, or recognisably machine-shaped. The goal is not to fool an AI detector—heuristics for identifying AI-written language are unreliable—but to make the writing clearer, more particular, and more recognisably owned by its writer.

## Revision principles

- Preserve the thesis, factual claims, intended audience, and overall emotional temperature unless the user asks for a deeper rewrite.
- Replace general claims with observable situations, concrete nouns, named actions, and specific consequences. Do not fabricate autobiographical details, sources, quotations, or statistics just to make prose feel human.
- Prefer active verbs and literal, precise wording. Turn unnecessary noun phrases into actions, remove bureaucratic padding, and keep jargon only when the idea needs it.
- Vary sentence length, openings, paragraph size, and transitions. Keep short sentences when they create emphasis; do not force uniform rhythm or add choppy fragments everywhere.
- Remove predictable scaffolding: repeated “there is” openings, stacked rhetorical questions, symmetrical lists, generic three-part climaxes, and paragraphs that all resolve with the same cadence.
- Keep useful repetition when it sounds intentional. A distinctive phrase or image can anchor an essay; repeated templates cannot.
- Let the writer make judgments. Use qualified claims where the evidence is limited, allow a little friction or uncertainty, and avoid smoothing every idea into a balanced conclusion.
- Read the revision aloud mentally. If every sentence sounds equally polished, revise for rhythm and emphasis rather than adding slang or errors.

## Workflow

1. State the article's central claim in one sentence before editing.
2. Mark passages that are abstract, interchangeable, over-explained, or rhythmically repetitive.
3. Rewrite the opening around a concrete scene, object, decision, or consequence when the source material supports one.
4. Compress explanation where an image or example can carry the idea. Keep the strongest conceptual turns.
5. Do a sentence-level pass for active verbs, clear referents, precise wording, and varied rhythm.
6. Check that the rewrite still sounds like one person making an argument, not a checklist of writing advice.
