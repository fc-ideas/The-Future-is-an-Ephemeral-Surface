# Sources for natural, human-sounding prose

This skill adapts general writing guidance rather than claiming that a detector can reliably identify AI text. The main source is Purdue University's Online Writing Lab (OWL), whose advice is useful for revision because it focuses on clarity, precision, sentence rhythm, and the reader's experience.

## Purdue OWL

- [Writing in Plain Style](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/writing_style/plain_style%20.html): use concise, action-driven sentences; prefer active voice when appropriate; choose literal, precise language; use verbs instead of nominalizations; build from familiar information toward new information; avoid unnecessary jargon.
- [Improving Sentence Clarity](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/mechanics/sentence_clarity.html): make the actor and action clear, avoid noun strings and unclear pronouns, and use parallel constructions when listing related ideas.
- [Strategies for Variation](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/sentence_variety/index.html): vary sentence length, structure, and openings so the prose has rhythm and emphasis rather than a monotonous pattern.

These pages do not prescribe a single voice. Apply their principles selectively: literary or conversational prose may intentionally use fragments, repetition, passive voice, jargon, or long sentences when those choices serve the piece.

## Important limitation

Do not describe the result as “undetectable,” “human-authored,” or guaranteed to evade an AI detector. Research by Jakesch, Hancock, and Naaman found that people were poor at detecting AI-generated self-presentations and that common human-versus-AI heuristics could be manipulated. See [Human heuristics for AI-generated language are flawed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07271).
